Glossary

Medicine price terms explained

Last reviewed 23 October 2026

Short answer: This page explains, in plain language, the words and abbreviations you meet when you look up medicine prices in Pakistan, such as MRP, S.R.O., DRAP, registration number and pack size. Use it as a quick reference while you read our other guides.

Price terms

TermWhat it means
MRP (maximum retail price)The highest price at which a medicine may be sold to the public. It is printed on the pack. A shop may charge less but should not charge more. See how DRAP sets medicine prices.
Retail priceThe price a customer pays at a pharmacy. It is the price printed on the pack.
Trade priceThe price at which a company sells to distributors and pharmacies, before the retail margin is added. It is lower than the retail price.
Price per tabletThe pack price divided by the number of tablets. It is the fairest way to compare packs. See pack sizes explained.
Effective from dateThe date a listed price came into force. A newer date means a newer price.
CPI (consumer price index)A measure of inflation. DRAP has issued some price notifications that adjust drug prices in line with it.

Official bodies and documents

TermWhat it means
DRAPThe Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan. It registers medicines and publishes official price notifications and a price list.
S.R.O.Statutory Regulatory Order. It is the form in which the government publishes an official notification, including notifications that fix or change medicine prices. Example: S.R.O. 207(I)/2023.
NotificationAn official published announcement. A price notification lists the medicines, strengths, pack sizes and the prices set for them.
ECCThe Economic Coordination Committee of the federal cabinet. News reports have said it approved some price increases, which DRAP then notified.
Drugs Act, 1976The law under which maximum retail prices of medicines are fixed.
PharmaPrice IndexDRAP's online list of registered products, with their registration numbers, manufacturers, pack sizes and prices.

Terms printed on the pack

TermWhat it means
Brand nameThe name a company gives its product. Many brands can contain the same ingredient.
Generic nameThe name of the active ingredient, for example paracetamol. See brand vs generic medicines.
Active ingredientThe part of the medicine that has the medical effect.
StrengthHow much active ingredient each tablet, capsule or measure contains, for example 500mg.
Dosage formThe form of the medicine, such as tablet, capsule, syrup, cream or injection.
Pack sizeHow many units are in a pack, for example 20 x 10's, which is 200 tablets.
Registration numberThe number under which the product is registered with DRAP. You can search for it on the PharmaPrice Index.
Batch numberA code that identifies the production batch. It helps trace a product if there is a problem.
Manufacturing date and expiry dateWhen the batch was made, and the date after which the medicine should not be used.

Terms about safety

TermWhat it means
Registered medicineA medicine that is registered with DRAP and can legally be sold.
Unregistered medicineA medicine sold without being registered with DRAP.
Counterfeit (fake) medicineA product deliberately made to look like a genuine brand, which may contain the wrong ingredient or amount.
Expired medicineA medicine past its expiry date, which should not be used.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between retail price and MRP?

The retail price is what you pay at the pharmacy. For medicines with a fixed maximum, the MRP is the highest that retail price may be. A shop can charge less than the MRP.

Where do I find the registration number on a pack?

It is printed on the label or carton, usually near the manufacturer's details. If you cannot find it, ask your pharmacist.

What does S.R.O. stand for?

It stands for Statutory Regulatory Order, which is how the government publishes official notifications, including medicine price notifications.
