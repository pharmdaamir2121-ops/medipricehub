A Guide to Common Over-the-Counter Medicines

Health Guides · Updated 2026

Over-the-counter (OTC) medicines are products you can buy without a prescription, used for everyday, self-limiting conditions. Knowing what a category is generally used for — and its basic buying considerations — can help you shop more confidently. This guide is educational only; it does not replace advice from a pharmacist or doctor for your specific situation.

Pain and fever relief

This category includes common analgesics used for headaches, minor aches, and fever. Products vary by active ingredient, strength, and formulation (tablets, syrups, effervescent forms). Always check the strength per tablet and the maximum daily amount stated on the label, and be aware that some pain relievers are combined with other ingredients like caffeine.

Antacids and digestive relief

Antacids and related products help with occasional heartburn, indigestion, or acidity. They differ in active ingredient (for example, aluminium/magnesium-based versus other formulations) and in whether they're a fast-acting liquid or a longer-lasting tablet. Persistent digestive symptoms are worth discussing with a doctor rather than treating long-term with OTC products alone.

Cold, cough and flu remedies

This is one of the most crowded OTC categories, with products targeting congestion, cough, sore throat, or a combination of symptoms. Combination products can contain several active ingredients at once, so it's worth checking labels carefully to avoid unintentionally doubling up on an ingredient if taking more than one product.

Allergy relief

Antihistamine-based products are commonly used for seasonal allergies, mild skin reactions, or hay-fever-type symptoms. Formulations vary in whether they cause drowsiness, which is worth checking if you need to drive or work afterward.

Formulation and dosing details vary by product and by individual health circumstances. Always read the package insert and consult a pharmacist if you're unsure which product or strength is appropriate — especially for children, during pregnancy, or alongside other medicines.

General buying considerations

  • Check the active ingredient, not just the brand name, especially if you're trying to avoid duplicating a medicine you already take.
  • Compare pack sizes rather than sticker price alone.
  • Check expiry dates, particularly for less commonly purchased items.
  • Buy from licensed pharmacies to reduce counterfeit risk — see our guide on spotting counterfeit medicines.
