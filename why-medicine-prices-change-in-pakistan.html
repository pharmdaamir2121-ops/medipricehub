Medicine Price Guide

Why Medicine Prices Change in Pakistan

Medicine prices can change for several reasons, including regulatory price revisions, inflation-linked adjustments, changes in product details, and differences between older and newer batches. Understanding these factors helps consumers compare prices more accurately.

Who regulates medicine prices?

The Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan (DRAP) regulates medicines and related products at the federal level. Its Drug Pricing Policy sets the framework for applicable medicine pricing, while the public PharmaPrice Index provides product-level pricing information.

Annual price adjustments

DRAP's pricing framework provides mechanisms for permitted annual MRP adjustments, including CPI-linked limits for relevant categories. The applicable limit depends on the category and rules in force, so consumers should check the specific product rather than assume every medicine changes by the same percentage.

Effective dates matter

The DRAP PharmaPrice Index includes an Effective From field. A price shown online may therefore relate to a particular effective date. When checking a medicine, compare the current pack and the applicable regulatory entry rather than relying on an undated old listing.

Older and newer batches

Medicines already manufactured before a price revision can create temporary differences in the market. This is one reason the MRP printed on the actual pack is important. Always check the batch and pack you are buying instead of assuming that an older online price applies to every current pack.

Pack size, strength and formulation

Price comparisons must use the exact product. Strength, dosage form and pack size can change the price. A 10-tablet pack should not be compared directly with a 20-tablet pack, even if the brand and active ingredient are the same.

Why can two pharmacies show different prices?

A difference can result from comparing different pack sizes, batches, product registrations, outdated online information, or a genuine discount. Before deciding that a price is incorrect, match the exact medicine details and check the MRP printed on the pack.

How to check whether a medicine price is current

  1. Match the brand/product name.
  2. Check the active ingredient and strength.
  3. Check dosage form and pack size.
  4. Check the registration number where available.
  5. Read the MRP printed on the actual pack.
  6. Search the product in the DRAP PharmaPrice Index.
  7. Compare the effective date and product details.

Why brand name alone is not enough

A brand family can contain several strengths or formulations. Two products with similar names may therefore have different approved prices. Reliable comparison requires matching the complete product information.

What if an online price does not match the pack?

First check that the online listing and pack describe the same strength, dosage form, pack size and registration. Then check the date. Online retail listings can become outdated, while the pack you are holding provides the MRP for that particular product.

Simple example

Suppose a website lists a medicine at Rs. 500 while the current pack shows an MRP of Rs. 550. Before treating the online listing as a better price, verify whether it refers to the same product and current information. A lower online figure may reflect an older listing or a discount rather than a different approved MRP.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do all medicines increase in price every year?

No. Price adjustments depend on the applicable regulatory category and rules.

Does every medicine increase by the same percentage?

No. Different categories can have different limits and conditions.

Is the DRAP PharmaPrice Index the same as a pharmacy's selling price?

No. It is DRAP's public regulatory price index. Retail promotions and observed selling prices can differ from an approved MRP, subject to applicable law.

Should I trust an old online medicine price?

Not without checking its date and exact product details. Use current regulatory information and the MRP on the pack.

What is the safest way to compare medicine prices?

Match the exact product first, then compare the current MRP and any clearly stated discount. Do not substitute a different strength or pack size simply because it is cheaper.
