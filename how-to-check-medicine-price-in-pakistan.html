How to Check the Price of a Medicine in Pakistan

Published: September 2026 • Information reviewed against current DRAP pricing resources

If you want to check the price of a medicine in Pakistan, start with the medicine pack and then verify the product against an official pricing source where available. The most useful details are the product name, strength, dosage form, pack size, registration number and printed MRP.

Quick answer: Check the printed MRP on the medicine pack, then use the Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan (DRAP) PharmaPrice Index to search the product by name or registration number. Match the manufacturer, pack size and other product details before treating two prices as a direct comparison. DRAP's index currently displays product name, registration number, manufacturer, category, pack size, price and effective date. citeturn0search0

1. Check the medicine pack first

The package is the first place to look because it identifies the exact product you are holding. Before comparing a price, record:

  • Brand or product name
  • Generic name or active ingredient
  • Strength, such as 500 mg
  • Dosage form, such as tablet, capsule, syrup or injection
  • Pack size, such as 10 tablets or 120 ml
  • Manufacturer
  • Registration number, if shown
  • Printed MRP

These details matter because two products can have similar names but different strengths, pack sizes or formulations.

2. Find the printed MRP

MRP means Maximum Retail Price. For medicine price checking, the printed MRP is an important consumer reference. Pakistan's current Drug Pricing Policy states that a drug should not be sold to a consumer above the MRP printed on the respective pack. citeturn0search0

If the price you are being asked to pay appears higher than the printed MRP, check the pack carefully and ask the seller to clarify the charge. Keep the receipt if you believe there is a pricing issue.

3. Search the DRAP PharmaPrice Index

DRAP provides a public PharmaPrice Index for pharmaceutical product pricing. The index includes a search facility and displays fields including product name, registration number, manufacturer, category, pack size, price and effective-from date. citeturn0search0

Open the DRAP PharmaPrice Index

Search by the product name first. If several results appear, use the registration number and manufacturer to identify the matching product.

4. Match the product details carefully

Finding a similar name is not enough. A correct price check should match the important product details.

5. Do not compare different pack sizes as if they were the same

A 10-tablet pack and a 20-tablet pack are not directly comparable simply because they contain the same active ingredient. Likewise, a 60 ml syrup should not be compared with a 120 ml bottle without considering the different quantity.

For a more useful comparison, calculate the price per tablet, capsule or millilitre when appropriate. Even then, make sure the strength and dosage form are equivalent.

6. Check the effective date

Medicine pricing information can change. DRAP's public index includes an Effective From field, and current records include dates from 2026 as well as older dates. citeturn0search0turn0search1

This means an old social-media post, price list, blog article or screenshot may show a price that is no longer current. When possible, use a current regulatory source and check the date attached to the record.

7. Understand the difference between an official listed price and a retail price

A regulatory pricing record and an observed online or pharmacy price are not necessarily the same type of information. A price-comparison website may also collect retail listings, discounts or reference prices.

MediPriceHub therefore aims to distinguish between DRAP-listed pricing information and observed/reference retail prices. This helps readers understand what kind of number they are seeing instead of treating every price as an official regulatory figure.

What if you cannot find the medicine in the DRAP index?

Not finding a product through one search does not by itself prove that a medicine is unregistered, unavailable or incorrectly priced. Try the exact product name, registration number or other identifying information shown on the pack. You can also check whether the product name has spelling or spacing differences.

If you still cannot verify the information, ask a licensed pharmacy or appropriately qualified healthcare professional for help with product identification. For regulatory questions, use DRAP's official resources.

What to do if a seller charges above the printed MRP

First, confirm that you are comparing the same product and pack and that the printed price is clearly visible. Keep the receipt or other purchase record. If you believe there is a regulatory violation, you can seek guidance through the relevant official authority rather than relying on an online price comparison alone.

Frequently asked questions

How can I check a medicine price online in Pakistan?

Start with the medicine pack, note the product and registration details, and search the DRAP PharmaPrice Index. Match the manufacturer, strength, dosage form and pack size before comparing prices. citeturn0search0

Is the DRAP PharmaPrice Index the same as a pharmacy's current selling price?

It is a regulatory pricing reference, not a guarantee of stock or an individual seller's online offer. The index provides product pricing information together with an effective date. citeturn0search0

Can I compare two medicines with the same generic name?

You can compare equivalent products, but check strength, dosage form, pack size, manufacturer and registration details first. A generic name alone is not enough for a precise price comparison.

Why is an old price different from today's price?

Pricing records can have different effective dates. Always check the date and source instead of relying on an undated historical price. citeturn0search0

Does checking the price tell me which medicine to take?

No. Price information does not determine whether a medicine is appropriate for a particular patient. Diagnosis, medicine selection, dose and duration should be determined by an appropriately qualified healthcare professional.

Simple medicine price-check checklist

  1. Read the exact product name.
  2. Check the active ingredient and strength.
  3. Check dosage form and pack size.
  4. Record the manufacturer and registration number.
  5. Read the printed MRP.
  6. Search the DRAP PharmaPrice Index.
  7. Match the product details and effective date.
  8. Compare like-for-like products only.
  9. Keep your receipt for purchases.
