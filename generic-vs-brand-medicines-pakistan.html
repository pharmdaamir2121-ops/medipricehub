Generic Medicine vs Brand Medicine in Pakistan: What's the Difference?

A practical consumer guide to active ingredients, registration, quality, price differences and safe medicine comparisons.

When people compare medicines in Pakistan, they often see several products containing the same active ingredient but sold under different brand names. Some may be relatively expensive while others cost less. Understanding the difference between a generic medicine and a brand medicine can make price comparisons easier—but price alone should not determine which medicine a person uses.

What is a generic medicine?

A generic medicine is a pharmaceutical product identified by its active ingredient rather than relying only on a particular brand name. For example, several manufacturers may market products containing the same active ingredient, strength and dosage form.

In Pakistan, medicines are subject to regulatory requirements overseen by the Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan (DRAP). DRAP states that pharmaceutical products are regulated for standards related to quality, safety and efficacy. Registration and interchangeability requirements can involve evidence such as pharmaceutical equivalence and, where applicable, bioequivalence studies.

What is a brand medicine?

A brand medicine is marketed under a specific product or trade name. The same active ingredient can be present in multiple brands made by different manufacturers. A brand name therefore does not, by itself, tell you that the product contains a different active ingredient.

Always check the medicine pack for the active ingredient, strength, dosage form, manufacturer and registration information rather than comparing names alone.

Are generic medicines the same as brand medicines?

It is important to be precise. Products with the same active ingredient are not automatically identical in every respect. DRAP's interchangeability guidance distinguishes pharmaceutical equivalence from therapeutic equivalence. Pharmaceutical products can contain the same active ingredient and dosage form while still requiring appropriate evidence before therapeutic equivalence or interchangeability can be established.

For a medicine that has been prescribed for you, do not switch products solely because another brand is cheaper. Ask a doctor or pharmacist if you are considering a substitution, particularly for medicines where product-specific clinical or bioequivalence considerations matter.

Why can prices be different?

Different products can have different approved prices and market prices. Pakistan's drug-pricing framework includes rules for maximum retail prices, and DRAP maintains a public PharmaPrice Index showing product name, registration number, manufacturer, pack size, price and effective date for listed products.

A lower price can therefore reflect a different brand, manufacturer, pack size, approved price or retailer discount. It does not by itself prove that one medicine is better or worse than another.

How to compare two medicines safely

  1. Write down the exact active ingredient.
  2. Compare the strength and dosage form.
  3. Compare the pack size or quantity.
  4. Check the manufacturer and registration details on the pack.
  5. Check the printed MRP and the price actually being offered.
  6. For regulatory price information, search the DRAP PharmaPrice Index.
  7. If the products are prescribed medicines, confirm any substitution with a qualified healthcare professional.

Example

Suppose two products both contain the same active ingredient and strength, but one pack contains 10 tablets and another contains 20. Comparing only the total pack price can be misleading. A useful comparison starts with the active ingredient, strength and dosage form, then considers quantity and the price per unit.

Does a cheaper medicine mean lower quality?

Not necessarily. Price is influenced by regulatory pricing, manufacturer, pack size, market conditions and discounts. A price difference alone is not evidence of a quality difference. Consumers should verify the product and purchase medicines through appropriate, legitimate channels.

Frequently asked questions

Is generic medicine legal in Pakistan?

Registered pharmaceutical products are regulated in Pakistan. Consumers should verify the product and its regulatory information rather than relying on the word “generic” alone.

Can I change from one brand to another?

For prescribed medicines, ask your doctor or pharmacist before changing brands. The decision can depend on the medicine, patient and clinical circumstances.

Where can I check medicine prices?

You can use the DRAP PharmaPrice Index for listed regulatory price information and compare it with the MRP printed on the actual pack and the price offered by the seller.

Why is the same medicine sold at different prices?

Possible reasons include different brands, pack sizes, approved prices, effective dates and retailer discounts. Always make a like-for-like comparison.
