How Medicine Prices Are Determined in Pakistan

Published: September 2026 • Information reviewed against current DRAP pricing resources

When people buy a medicine in Pakistan, they may see a printed maximum retail price (MRP), a pharmacy selling price, or a discounted price. Understanding what these figures mean makes it easier to compare products without confusing a regulated price with an individual seller's offer.

Quick answer: Medicine pricing in Pakistan is subject to regulatory rules and approved pricing information. The Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan (DRAP) publishes a PharmaPrice Index containing product, registration, manufacturer, pack-size, price and effective-date information. The printed MRP on a medicine pack is important when checking the maximum consumer price.

Who regulates medicine prices in Pakistan?

The Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan (DRAP) is the federal authority responsible for regulation of therapeutic goods, including medicines. One of the public resources provided by DRAP is its PharmaPrice Index, where users can search pharmaceutical product pricing information.

The current DRAP index displays fields such as product name, registration number, manufacturer, category, pack size, price and the date from which the listed price is effective. This makes the index useful as a reference when researching medicine prices.

Check the DRAP PharmaPrice Index

What does MRP mean?

MRP means Maximum Retail Price. In Pakistan's drug-pricing framework, MRP is the maximum retail price fixed under the applicable regulatory framework. The current Drug Pricing Policy states that a drug should not be sold to a consumer above the MRP printed on its pack.

The same policy also addresses medicines sold in loose quantities: the MRP should be calculated on a pro-rata basis from the MRP printed on the relevant pack.

Read the DRAP Drug Pricing Policy

How is a medicine's price established?

Medicine pricing is not simply a matter of a shop choosing any number it wants. The regulatory framework provides procedures for fixing and regulating MRPs. The applicable process can depend on the medicine, its category and the regulatory provisions in force at the time.

For consumers, the most practical point is that an approved price and the printed pack information provide a reference for checking what a medicine should cost at retail. Price records can also have an effective from date, so an older price should not automatically be treated as the current one.

Essential medicines and other medicines

Pakistan's pricing framework distinguishes medicines included in the National Essential Medicines List from other medicines. The Drug Pricing Policy describes procedures for fixing and regulating MRPs and provides specific treatment for medicines covered by the essential-medicines framework.

This distinction matters because two products with different regulatory classifications may not follow exactly the same pricing pathway. Consumers should therefore avoid assuming that every medicine is priced using one simple formula.

Why can two medicines with the same active ingredient have different prices?

A generic name identifies an active ingredient, but products can differ in manufacturer, strength, dosage form, pack size, registration status, formulation details and approved pricing history. A 10-tablet pack and a 20-tablet pack, for example, cannot be compared simply by looking at the price printed on each pack.

When comparing medicines, check at least these details:

  • Brand or product name
  • Active ingredient or generic name
  • Strength
  • Dosage form, such as tablet, capsule, syrup or injection
  • Pack size
  • Registration number, when available
  • Printed MRP and expiry information

Why does the price sometimes change?

Approved medicine prices can change when a new price becomes effective under the applicable regulatory process. DRAP's public PharmaPrice Index therefore includes an effective date with listed pricing information.

For example, the current index contains entries with effective dates in 2026 as well as older entries. This is why a price seen in an old article, advertisement, invoice or social-media post may not be a reliable indication of the current approved price.

MRP, trade price and the price you pay

These terms should not be treated as interchangeable.

TermWhat it generally means
MRPThe maximum retail price printed on the medicine pack under the applicable pricing framework.
Trade priceA distribution-level price concept defined in the pricing framework; it should not be confused with the consumer-facing MRP.
Retail selling priceThe amount a consumer is actually charged by a seller, subject to applicable law and the product's MRP.
Discounted priceA seller's reduced price or promotional offer, where permitted and genuinely available.

Because commercial discounts and availability can vary, MediPriceHub treats a regulatory listed price differently from an observed online retail price when displaying database information.

How to check a medicine price safely

  1. Check the pack. Look for the printed MRP, product name, strength and pack size.
  2. Check the expiry date. Do not purchase or use a medicine that has expired.
  3. Check the product identity. Make sure the brand, strength and dosage form match what you intend to compare.
  4. Search the DRAP PharmaPrice Index. Where an entry is available, compare the product and registration details with the pack.
  5. Compare like with like. A different strength or pack size is not a direct price comparison.
  6. Use authorized sellers. For prescription medicines, follow the advice of an appropriately qualified healthcare professional and use a licensed/authorized source.

Frequently asked questions

Is the price on MediPriceHub guaranteed?

No. Price information can change and sellers may have different availability or lawful discounts. Always check the medicine pack and the current source before making a purchase.

Is a generic medicine always cheaper?

Not necessarily. Price depends on the specific product, approved pricing information, strength, pack size and other factors. Compare equivalent products rather than assuming that a generic label automatically means the lowest price.

Can a pharmacy charge more than the printed MRP?

The current Drug Pricing Policy states that a person including a retailer, hospital, clinic, wholesaler or distributor should not sell a drug to a consumer above the MRP printed on the respective pack.

Why is the price on an old website different?

Medicine prices can be revised and new prices can have a later effective date. Always check the date and source rather than relying on an undated price list.

Does price comparison tell me which medicine I should take?

No. Price comparison is not a substitute for diagnosis or prescribing advice. The appropriate medicine, dose and duration depend on the patient's condition and should be determined by an appropriately qualified healthcare professional.
