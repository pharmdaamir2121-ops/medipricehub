Understanding Your Prescription: Key Terms Explained

Health Guides · Updated 2026

Prescriptions and medicine packaging use terms that can feel unfamiliar. This glossary explains the most common ones in plain language, so you can read a label or prescription with more confidence — and ask better questions at the pharmacy if something's unclear.

Glossary

Generic name
The name of the active ingredient itself, independent of any brand. For example, "paracetamol" is a generic name that appears under many different brand names.
Brand name
The trademarked name a manufacturer gives its version of a medicine. Different brands can share the same generic ingredient.
Strength
The amount of active ingredient in each unit (tablet, capsule, or ml of liquid), usually shown in milligrams (mg) or micrograms (mcg). Always check this matches what was prescribed — the same brand often comes in multiple strengths.
Pack size
How many units (tablets, capsules, ml) are in the box or bottle. Useful for comparing total cost, not just sticker price.
Dosage / directions for use
Instructions on how much to take, how often, and for how long. These should always come from your doctor or the package insert — never estimated.
Batch / lot number
A code identifying the specific production batch. Useful for tracing a product if there's ever a recall or quality concern.
Expiry date
The date after which the manufacturer no longer guarantees the medicine's full effectiveness and safety. Medicines should generally not be used after this date.
Registration number
A number issued by DRAP (Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan) confirming a medicine is registered and legally approved for sale in Pakistan.
Route of administration
How the medicine is meant to be taken — for example, oral (by mouth), topical (on the skin), or injectable.
Active vs. inactive ingredients
The active ingredient produces the therapeutic effect. Inactive ingredients (fillers, coatings, flavoring) help with manufacturing, stability, or taste but aren't intended to treat anything themselves.
If any term on your prescription or medicine box is unclear, ask your pharmacist directly — that's exactly what they're there for, and it only takes a moment.

Why this matters when comparing prices

Understanding these terms helps you compare medicines correctly. Two products with the same brand name but different strengths are not interchangeable, and comparing prices only makes sense between products with matching generic name, strength, and pack size.
