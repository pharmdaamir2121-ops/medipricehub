How to Compare Medicine Prices Safely in Pakistan

Comparing medicine prices can help consumers make informed decisions, but a useful comparison must be made between the same or equivalent product details. Brand, active ingredient, strength, dosage form and pack size can all affect price.

1. Start with the exact medicine

Before comparing prices, check the brand or product name, active ingredient, strength, dosage form and pack size. A 10-tablet pack should not be compared directly with a 20-tablet pack.

2. Check the strength

Medicines may be available in different strengths. Compare products with the same strength when your goal is to compare the price of the same product.

3. Check the pack size

Pack size can substantially affect the total price. Record the number of tablets, capsules, millilitres or other units before comparing two listings.

4. Understand MRP and discounts

The maximum retail price (MRP) printed on a medicine pack is different from a temporary retail discount. When a seller advertises a discount, check the original MRP and the final selling price.

5. Check the actual pack

Look at the printed MRP, batch details and expiry date on the pack you are considering. Online listings can become outdated, so an old web price should not automatically be treated as the current price.

6. Use reliable regulatory information

DRAP's public PharmaPrice Index provides product name, registration number, manufacturer, category, pack size, price and effective-date information. Use these details to make sure an online record matches the product you are comparing.

Open the DRAP PharmaPrice Index.

7. Compare like with like

  • Same active ingredient
  • Same strength
  • Same dosage form
  • Same pack size
  • Same or correctly matched product/registration information

8. Do not choose a medicine only because it is cheaper

Price is only one consideration. Do not switch a prescribed medicine, strength or formulation solely because another product costs less. Ask a qualified healthcare professional when you are unsure whether products are appropriate substitutes.

9. A simple comparison example

Suppose one listing shows Rs. 450 and another shows Rs. 500. Before choosing the lower price, check whether both products have the same strength, dosage form and pack size, and whether the prices refer to the same current product.

10. Safe comparison checklist

  1. Match the medicine name and active ingredient.
  2. Match the strength and dosage form.
  3. Match the pack size.
  4. Check the printed MRP.
  5. Check expiry and packaging.
  6. Check DRAP price information and effective date where available.
  7. Compare the final selling price, including any clearly stated discount.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can two pharmacies have different prices?

Different listings can reflect discounts, different products, pack sizes, batches or outdated information. Verify the exact product before comparing.

Is the cheapest medicine always the right choice?

No. Price alone does not establish that two medicines are interchangeable or suitable for an individual.

Where can I check medicine pricing information?

The DRAP PharmaPrice Index is a useful official source for regulatory price information. You should also check the MRP printed on the actual pack.

Why does pack size matter?

A larger pack can have a higher total price. Comparing total prices without accounting for quantity can give a misleading result.
