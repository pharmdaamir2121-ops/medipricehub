Why Medicine Prices Differ in Pakistan

Health Guides · Updated 2026

If you've ever compared two boxes of what seems like the "same" medicine and found very different prices, you're not imagining things. Price differences for medicines in Pakistan are common, and understanding why can help you make more informed, safer purchasing decisions — without assuming the cheaper option is worse or the pricier one is better.

Brand-name vs. generic pricing

Most medicines contain an active ingredient (the generic name) that can be manufactured and sold under many different brand names. A company that first develops or heavily markets a drug often prices it higher, while other manufacturers producing the same active ingredient may price it lower. Both can be legitimate, registered products — the difference is branding, marketing cost, and sometimes formulation details like coating or release mechanism.

Manufacturer and production costs

Different manufacturers have different production scales, import costs for raw materials, and quality-control processes, all of which affect final price. Imported raw materials tied to currency exchange rates can also cause price movement over time, even for the same product.

Where DRAP price regulation fits in

Pakistan's Drug Regulatory Authority (DRAP) sets and monitors maximum retail prices for many registered medicines through mechanisms like the PharmaPrice Index. This regulatory price acts as a ceiling — a pharmacy should not charge more than the DRAP-notified price for a given pack. In practice, actual shelf prices can still vary slightly below that ceiling depending on the seller.

On MediPriceHub, we label prices as either DRAP-regulated or reference/observed so you know which type of figure you're looking at. Reference prices are collected where a directly matching regulatory record wasn't available, and should always be verified with the seller before purchase.

Pack size and quantity

A common source of confusion is comparing prices across different pack sizes. A strip of 10 tablets will naturally cost less than a box of 100, even if the per-tablet price is identical or higher. Always check the pack size field, not just the sticker price, before concluding one product is cheaper.

What this means for you as a buyer

  • Compare the same generic ingredient and strength, not just the brand name.
  • Check the pack size before comparing totals.
  • Prefer purchasing from licensed pharmacies who follow DRAP-notified pricing.
  • Remember that a lower price alone doesn't confirm authenticity — always check packaging and seals.
