How to Spot Counterfeit Medicines

Health Guides · Updated 2026

Counterfeit and substandard medicines are a real concern in many markets, including Pakistan. They can look almost identical to genuine products but may contain the wrong dose, no active ingredient at all, or unsafe substitutes. Knowing what to check before you buy — and after — can reduce your risk significantly.

Buy from licensed, traceable sources

The single biggest protection is where you buy. Licensed pharmacies that source from authorized distributors are far less likely to carry counterfeit stock than unregulated street vendors, informal online sellers, or unusually cheap bulk deals. If a deal seems far below the DRAP-notified price for that product, treat it as a red flag rather than a bargain.

Packaging checklist

  • Print quality: Blurry text, faded colors, or misspelled words on the box are warning signs.
  • Batch number and expiry: These should be clearly printed and legible, not handwritten or stickered over other information.
  • Seals and blister packs: Look for intact security seals. Damaged, re-glued, or missing seals are a concern.
  • Holograms and security features: Many manufacturers include holograms or QR verification codes — check these against the manufacturer's official guidance if available.
  • Consistency with past purchases: If a product you've bought before suddenly looks or tastes different, ask the pharmacist directly.
If something seems off, don't take the medicine. Return it to the point of sale, and consider reporting it to DRAP through their official reporting channels. Reporting helps protect other consumers too.

Registration number verification

Legitimate medicines sold in Pakistan carry a DRAP registration number. You can cross-check a product's registration status through DRAP's official resources. If a product has no registration number at all, or one that doesn't match when you search, that's a serious red flag.

Price as a signal, not proof

An unusually low price compared to the DRAP-regulated price or typical market rate can be one clue among several — but price alone doesn't confirm a product is fake, and a normal price doesn't guarantee authenticity either. Use it alongside the packaging and source checks above, not as a standalone test.

What to do if you suspect a counterfeit

  1. Stop using the product immediately.
  2. Keep the packaging and receipt.
  3. Contact the pharmacy where you purchased it.
  4. Report to DRAP's official complaint channel.
  5. If you've already taken the medicine and feel unwell, seek medical attention.
