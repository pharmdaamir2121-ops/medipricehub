MRP vs Retail Price vs Discounted Price in Pakistan

Published: September 2026 • Information reviewed against current DRAP pricing resources

When you buy a medicine, you may see several different numbers: the MRP printed on the pack, a pharmacy's selling price, a discount price, or a price shown on a website. These terms can be confusing. Understanding what each number means can help you compare medicine prices more accurately.

Quick answer: MRP means Maximum Retail Price and is the maximum price printed on the pack for retail sale. A discounted selling price may be lower than the MRP. Pakistan's Drug Pricing Policy states that a drug should not be sold to a consumer above the MRP printed on the respective pack. Always compare the same product, strength, dosage form and pack size. Read the DRAP Drug Pricing Policy.

1. What is MRP?

MRP stands for Maximum Retail Price. It is the maximum retail price printed on the medicine pack. DRAP's Drug Pricing Policy states that no person, including a retailer, hospital, clinic, wholesaler or distributor, should sell a drug to a consumer at a price exceeding the MRP printed on the respective pack.

For loose quantities, the policy also addresses the pro-rata MRP based on the printed pack price. This is important when comparing a partial quantity with a complete pack.

2. What is the retail selling price?

The retail selling price is the amount a consumer actually pays a pharmacy or other retail seller for the medicine. It can be equal to or lower than the printed MRP when a lawful discount or price reduction is offered.

Do not assume that every online price is an official DRAP price. Websites may display their own retail listings, promotions or reference prices. Check the source and the date before treating a price as regulatory information.

3. What is a discounted price?

A discounted price is a selling price reduced from a reference or listed price. For example, if a pack has an MRP of Rs 500 and a seller offers it for Rs 450, the difference is Rs 50 and the consumer pays Rs 450.

4. MRP is not the same as trade price

Pakistan's Drug Pricing Policy also defines a trade price. In the policy, trade price is described as the price after deducting the retailer discount from the maximum retail price fixed under the relevant framework. This is a supply-chain concept and should not be confused with the final amount a consumer pays at a pharmacy.

Consumers generally need to focus on the printed MRP and the actual retail price they are being asked to pay, rather than trying to use a trade price as a consumer price.

5. Why two websites can show different medicine prices

Different prices do not automatically mean that one listing is wrong. The listings may represent different things:

  • One may show the printed MRP.
  • Another may show a discounted retail price.
  • A third may show a reference or observed market price.
  • The products may have different pack sizes or strengths.
  • The price records may have different effective dates.
  • One listing may be old while another is more recent.

That is why a useful comparison must identify the exact product before comparing the numbers.

6. Check the DRAP price record

The DRAP PharmaPrice Index provides public pharmaceutical product pricing information. Its records include product name, registration number, manufacturer, category, pack size, price and effective-from date.

Open the DRAP PharmaPrice Index

When using the index, match the product name, manufacturer, strength or formulation, pack size and registration information where available. The effective date is also important because pricing records can change over time.

7. Example: how to compare a medicine price

Suppose a medicine pack has a printed MRP of Rs 800. A pharmacy sells the same pack for Rs 760. Another website advertises Rs 720.

  • Rs 800 is the printed MRP in this example.
  • Rs 760 is the first seller's retail price.
  • Rs 720 is the advertised price on the second website.

Before deciding that Rs 720 is the better price, confirm that all three listings refer to the same manufacturer, strength, dosage form, pack size and current product. Also check whether the website's price is actually available to you and whether delivery or other charges apply.

8. How to calculate the discount

If the MRP is Rs 800 and the selling price is Rs 720:

Saving = Rs 800 − Rs 720 = Rs 80

Discount percentage = (Rs 80 ÷ Rs 800) × 100 = 10%

This calculation describes the price difference. It does not by itself confirm that the product is authentic, available, appropriately stored or suitable for your medical needs.

9. What if a seller charges more than the printed MRP?

First, make sure you have identified the correct pack and that the printed price is clear. Check the receipt and ask the seller to explain the charge. If you believe a seller has charged more than the permitted MRP, keep the purchase record and use the relevant official regulatory channel for guidance or complaint handling.

Visit the Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan

10. What if the price is much lower than MRP?

A low price can result from a legitimate discount, promotion or other commercial reason. Price alone is not proof that a medicine is counterfeit or unsafe. However, consumers should still check the packaging, expiry date, batch information, registration details where applicable and the seller's credibility.

For prescription medicines, price comparison should never replace advice from a qualified healthcare professional.

11. A simple checklist before buying

  • Check the medicine name and active ingredient.
  • Check strength and dosage form.
  • Check the pack size.
  • Check the printed MRP.
  • Check the expiry date and packaging condition.
  • Compare the actual selling price with the MRP.
  • Check the source and date of any online price.
  • For regulatory verification, consult the DRAP PharmaPrice Index where applicable.
  • Keep your receipt for important purchases.

Frequently asked questions

Can a pharmacy sell a medicine above the printed MRP?

DRAP's Drug Pricing Policy states that a drug should not be sold to a consumer at a price exceeding the MRP printed on the respective pack.

Is a discounted price always the official DRAP price?

No. A discounted price is a seller's retail offer. DRAP's PharmaPrice Index is a separate regulatory pricing information source.

Why should I check pack size?

Because a 10-tablet pack and a 20-tablet pack can have different prices even when they contain the same medicine and strength. Comparing them without accounting for quantity can give a misleading result.

Should I choose a medicine because it is cheaper?

Price is only one consideration. Medicine selection should be based on the condition being treated, the prescribed or appropriate product, safety, authenticity and advice from a qualified healthcare professional when needed.
