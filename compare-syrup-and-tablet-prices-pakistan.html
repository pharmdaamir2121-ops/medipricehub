How to compare syrup, drops and tablet prices fairly

Last reviewed 20 November 2026

Short answer: Compare a syrup only with another syrup of the same medicine and the same strength per 5 ml. Divide the price by the number of millilitres, and think about how long an opened bottle lasts. Do not compare a syrup with a tablet by price alone, and do not switch between them to save money without asking your doctor or pharmacist.

Why liquids are harder to compare

With tablets, you can divide the price by the number of tablets. With liquids, two things change at once: the size of the bottle and the strength of the medicine in each measure. A bigger bottle is not always better value, and a cheaper bottle may simply contain a weaker syrup.

Step 1: Check the strength per measure

Syrup labels usually give the strength per 5 ml, for example "100mg/5ml". Two syrups with the same ingredient can have different strengths. If the strength is not the same, they are not the same product, and a price comparison is not fair. See how to read a medicine label.

Step 2: Work out the price per millilitre

Divide the price by the volume of the bottle in millilitres.

Step 3: Think about how long an opened bottle lasts

Many liquid medicines have a limited life once opened, and the label or leaflet may say how many days. A large bottle that is cheaper per ml is not good value if you will have to throw part of it away. Buy the size that matches what you will use before the opened bottle expires. Syrup against tablet: why a price comparison can mislead

A syrup and a tablet of the same medicine are different products. You cannot tell which is better value just from the price, because:

  • The amount of medicine in a measure of syrup is different from the amount in a tablet.
  • The right dose depends on the person, such as a child's age and weight, and a doctor or pharmacist decides it.
  • A syrup may be the only suitable form for a young child or for someone who cannot swallow tablets, whatever it costs.
  • Syrups often cost more for the same amount of medicine. That is often because they are made for people who cannot take tablets, but the price does not tell you whether a product is suitable.

For this reason, never change from one form to another, or change how much you give, to save money. Ask your doctor or pharmacist. This is especially important for children.

Drops, sachets and other liquids

  • Drops (for the eyes, ears, nose or by mouth): compare only the same medicine, the same strength and the same bottle volume. Check how long the bottle is safe to use after opening.
  • Powders for mixing (sachets or powders made up with water): compare the price per sachet or per dose, and check how long the mixed liquid keeps.
  • Always use the measuring cup, spoon or dropper supplied. Kitchen spoons vary in size, and an incorrect measure can mean too much or too little.

A quick checklist

  1. Is it the same medicine, with the same strength per 5 ml?
  2. Is the volume of each bottle known?
  3. Have I divided the price by the volume?
  4. Will I use the bottle before it expires after opening?
  5. Have I checked the expiry date, the registration and the printed price?
  6. Have I asked my pharmacist if I'm unsure? Our list of 10 questions to ask your pharmacist will help.

Frequently asked questions

Is a bigger bottle always better value?

Not always. A bigger bottle usually costs less per ml, but if part of it expires before you use it, you lose the saving. Check how long an opened bottle lasts.

Can I use a kitchen spoon to measure syrup?

It is safer to use the measuring cup, spoon or dropper that comes with the medicine, because kitchen spoons vary in size.

Can I give my child a crushed tablet instead of syrup to save money?

Do not change the form or the dose without asking a doctor or pharmacist. Some tablets should not be crushed, and a child's dose depends on age and weight.

Why does my syrup cost more than the tablets?

Different forms are priced differently. The price does not show which one suits you, so ask your pharmacist rather than choosing by price.
