How Seasonal Illness Affects Medicine Prices & Availability

Health Guides · Updated 2026

Certain times of year bring predictable spikes in demand for specific medicines — flu season, monsoon-related illnesses like dengue, and allergy seasons all shift what pharmacies sell most. Understanding these patterns can help you plan ahead rather than scrambling when demand is highest.

Flu and cold season

During cooler months, demand for cold and flu remedies, pain and fever relief, and cough medicines typically rises. Increased demand doesn't usually change DRAP-regulated prices directly, but it can occasionally affect short-term availability of specific brands or pack sizes at individual pharmacies.

Monsoon and dengue season

During monsoon months, products related to fever management and, in some regions, platelet-support supplements see higher demand due to dengue and other mosquito-borne illnesses. This is also a period where public health awareness campaigns are common — worth following official guidance from health authorities.

Allergy seasons

Pollen and seasonal allergy triggers vary by region, and antihistamine demand often follows local seasonal patterns rather than a single national calendar.

How this affects prices and buying decisions

  • DRAP-regulated maximum prices don't change simply because of seasonal demand, but temporary local shortages can occur.
  • Reference or observed retail prices (where no exact regulatory match exists) may show more variation during high-demand periods.
  • Buying only what you reasonably need — rather than stockpiling — helps keep supply available for everyone.
If you notice a pharmacy charging above the DRAP-notified price during a high-demand period, that's worth raising directly with the pharmacy, and can be reported to DRAP if it persists.

Planning ahead

If you or a family member regularly needs a specific medicine during a particular season (for example, allergy medicine every spring), it can help to check availability slightly ahead of the season rather than waiting until symptoms start and demand peaks.
